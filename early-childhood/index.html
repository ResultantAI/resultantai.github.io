This fall specifically

Two things are happening at once, and both are about how you are perceived

Confirm current dates on ECLKC before planning around them, because this is moving.

The Performance Standards are being rewritten
ACF published a proposed rule in August 2026 that would rescind and replace the Head Start Program Performance Standards, touching health and safety, staffing, eligibility, comprehensive services, parent engagement and governance. Current standards remain in effect during rulemaking.
Recompetition is opening
The Office of Head Start funding forecast anticipates the next round of competitive funding opportunities in October 2026, with applications due in December. That is a short window landing on top of everything else in your fall.
Why a vendor is telling you this
A competitive application is a diligence document. It argues that you are a high quality organization worth five more years. Everything a reviewer, a funder or a partner can find about you outside that document either supports the argument or quietly undercuts it. Most organizations have never checked which.

What is running today

A content review bottleneck, rebuilt in sixty days

The organization
An early childhood services organization that supports Head Start programs. The executive director was the buyer and the pilot sponsor, which is the pattern that works: one senior owner, one process, a fixed window.
The problem
Content going out to programs had to be reviewed by hand before it could be used. Creation was not the constraint. Review was.
What was built
A content scoring system on Softr, Airtable and Make. Submitted content is scored against the review criteria, then routed to a human reviewer with the score and the reasoning attached. The reviewer approves, edits or rejects. Content architecture was restructured and the asset library moved to AWS S3 as part of the same engagement.
Why human in the loop, plainly
Content reaching programs and families carries compliance weight. The system removes the reading load from the reviewer and leaves the judgment with them. It was never designed to approve anything on its own, and it did not replace a reviewer.

Questions

What directors ask first

Does anything reach a family without a person approving it?
No. Human in the loop is a constraint, not a setting, and it is not configurable away.
Do you work with parents as end users?
Parent engagement is a statutory requirement with a governance role attached, not a UX problem. I build for the staff who support families, not around the families.
We are a services arm, not a grantee. Does that change anything?
Yes, and it is worth saying out loud early. A program arm and a services arm have different buyers, different competitive sets and different compliance surfaces, and most organizations have never written that distinction down. Which one is contracting changes what gets built.
What does a first engagement look like?
One process, one senior sponsor, sixty days, fixed fee. Workflow automation engagements start at $5,000.
What do you not do?
Nothing that touches child assessment, eligibility determination, or anything a monitoring review would look at as a program decision. The line is administrative load, and I stay on my side of it.
